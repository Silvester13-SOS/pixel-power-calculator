How close together your pixels are on the wire or strip. Stock bullet-pixel harnesses are 4 in (10 cm); dense strip is 60 px/m.
100%
The output cap your show runs at — i.e. xLights Max Brightness or the equivalent in your sequencer. This is the ceiling if a sustained all-white scene ever fires. Most shows cap at 60–80%. RWU below handles the "how much of that cap do I actually average" question.
45%
Think of it this way:
• Brightness = the cap your show is allowed to hit.
• RWU = what fraction of that cap your sequences actually average.

Colourful xLights animation typically averages 25–45% because pixels cycle through single colours instead of holding solid white. Static-white or pixel-mapped video sits at 60–80%. All-white test pattern = 100%.
Results show both peak (safety-planning) and RWU (realistic) voltage drop.

A short wire (1–3 ft) exiting the controller or PSU enclosure. Turn on only if you have one.

Longer runs need heavier (lower AWG number) wire. 12–14 AWG is typical for 10–30 ft runs.
Total feet from the PSU/controller to the first pixel. Measure the actual run, not straight-line.
When off, the tool assumes the pigtail/extension carries the full strand's current (single-port, no injection). Turn on to model a real install where injection taps bypass the pigtail.
Loop method: one feed cable (often a 4-conductor) carries +/− to the string start, and the same cable's extra pair continues alongside the string and injects +/− at the far end. Treated as double-ended injection with one tap at the last pixel. Midpoint sees the highest voltage drop.
For loop method, this is the full length of the end-feed wire from PSU to the last pixel — i.e. the run from the PSU out to the string plus the length along the string. If the feed cable is 25 ft and the string is 50 ft long, enter 75.
⚠ If your PSU does not have an unused V+/V- output pair and you double up connections on a single screw terminal set, you are doing so at your own risk — this is generally not advised.
